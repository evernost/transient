Predistorted VCA test rig, Rev B

The same cell as Rev A, with both resistor feeds replaced by transistor current sources. The diode current and the tail current no longer follow the signal, so the 2nd harmonic drops from 1.2 % to under 0.01 % at 8 Vpp, and the gain control now goes all the way to zero.

For voltage control, replace RV1 and R15 with an op-amp that puts a CV between −15 V and −5 V on Q6's base. The gain then follows the CV in a straight line, starting from zero at −14.35 V.

Bring-up and trim

  1. Short IN to ground, close SW1 and turn RV1 fully toward R15 (maximum tail current).

  2. Measure across R1. About 1.15 V means 0.95 mA. LEDs vary: if yours gives a different reading, change R1 to that voltage divided by 0.95 mA.

  3. Check the other DC levels against the table. If COL1 and COL2 differ by more than about 0.3 V, the pair is mismatched by more than 2 mV: swap parts before trimming.

  4. Adjust RV2 until OUT reads 0 V DC.

  5. Turn RV1 through its range. OUT should stay within a few millivolts of zero, because every mismatch here is a current-ratio error that scales with the tail current. The first 5 % or so of travel at the −15 V end does nothing: Q6 needs 0.65 V to turn on.

  6. Feed a 4 Vpp, 1 kHz sine. At maximum gain OUT should be about 2.5 Vpp.

Experiments

  1. Diodes in and out. Keep the 4 Vpp input and open SW1. Q3 and Q4 switch off and the bases see IN ÷ 11 directly. Q5 has nowhere to send its current and saturates, which does no harm. Turn your monitor down first.

    Small-signal gain jumps to about 10, and the 4 Vpp sine becomes a squashed square, about 26 % THD. The bare pair is clean only below about 0.3 Vpp. RV2 shifts its balance, so re-centre by eye while SW1 is open and re-trim after closing it.

  2. See the predistortion. Probe TP1 and TP2 on two channels and display TP1 − TP2.

    About 43 mVpp at 8 Vpp in, with stretched, pointed peaks: the inverse of the pair's tanh curve. OUT stays a clean sine.

  3. Gain law, down to zero. Sweep RV1 from end to end.

    The output follows Q6's base voltage in a straight line and fades to silence. The waveshape doesn't change: 0.08 % THD at 8 Vpp whether the gain is 0.63, 0.3 or 0.03.

  4. Temperature. Before you glue the four together, hold a fingertip on Q1 alone.

    OUT drifts as Q1's VBE falls by about 2 mV per °C. This is why the four are glued together.

  5. Warmth control. From the diodes' top node (below SW1) to ground, wire a 22 µF capacitor (+ toward the node), 1k and a 50k linear pot as a variable resistor, all in series.

    The capacitor keeps DC out, so the bias and the gain stay put. The input moves that node by about 360 mVpp at 8 Vpp, and the resistor turns the movement into a wobble in ID, which is 2nd harmonic. At 8 Vpp: about 0.2 % with the pot at maximum, 1.7 % with 4.7k in circuit, 6 % at minimum.

  6. Colour swaps. Replace Q3 and Q4 with 1N4148s, anodes to the top node. Separately, raise R1.

    The 1N4148s give a gain of about 1.1 and a soft, symmetric compression: about 3 % THD at 8 Vpp, nearly all odd harmonics. Raising R1 lowers ID, which raises the gain and lowers the clip point. With 2.2k: gain 1.1, clip near ±5 V. With 4.7k: gain 2, clip near ±2.5 V. A 4.7k pot in series with R1 makes a drive control.
