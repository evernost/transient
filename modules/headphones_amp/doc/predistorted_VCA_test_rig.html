Predistorted VCA test rig

A BJT differential pair whose input is first warped by two diode-connected transistors. The diodes take the logarithm of the input current split, the pair takes the exponential, and the two cancel, so the pair accepts volts instead of millivolts. SW1 takes the diodes out so you can hear what they do.

Bring-up and trim

  1. Short IN to ground, close SW1 and turn RV1 to minimum resistance (maximum tail current).

  2. Check the DC levels against the table. If COL1 and COL2 differ by more than about 0.3 V, the pair is mismatched by more than 2 mV: swap parts before trimming.

  3. Adjust RV2 until OUT reads 0 V DC.

  4. Turn RV1 through its range. OUT should stay within a few millivolts of zero. Every mismatch in this circuit is a current-ratio error that scales with the tail current, so one trim holds across the whole gain range. A slow drift means the four transistors haven't settled to the same temperature.

  5. Feed a 4 Vpp, 1 kHz sine. At maximum gain OUT should be about 2.8 Vpp.

Experiments

  1. Diodes in and out. Keep the 4 Vpp input and open SW1. Q3 and Q4 switch off and the bases see IN ÷ 11 directly. Turn your monitor down first.

    Small-signal gain jumps to about 10, and the 4 Vpp sine becomes a squashed square, about 26 % THD. The bare pair is clean only below about 0.3 Vpp. RV2 shifts its balance, so re-centre by eye while SW1 is open and re-trim after closing it.

  2. See the predistortion. Probe TP1 and TP2 on two channels and display TP1 − TP2.

    About 44 mVpp at 8 Vpp in, with stretched, pointed peaks: the inverse of the pair's tanh curve. OUT stays a clean sine.

  3. Gain law. Halve the tail current with RV1.

    The output halves and the waveshape doesn't change. Gain is proportional to IT, and distortion is the same at every gain setting.

  4. Temperature. Before you glue the four together, hold a fingertip on Q1 alone.

    OUT drifts as Q1's VBE falls by about 2 mV per °C. This is why the four are glued together.

  5. Remove the 2nd harmonic. Lift R5 from ground and drive it from U1B wired as a unity-gain inverter (100k in, 100k feedback) fed from IN.

    The base nodes stop moving together, so R1's current stays constant. The 2nd harmonic falls from about 1.2 % to under 0.01 % at 8 Vpp, and gain doubles.

  6. Colour swaps. Replace Q3 and Q4 with 1N4148s, anodes to the top node. Separately, raise R1 to 22k–47k.

    The 1N4148s give a gain of about 1.2 and a soft compression, about 3 % THD at 8 Vpp. A larger R1 lowers ID: more gain and an earlier clip, so R1 works like a drive control.
